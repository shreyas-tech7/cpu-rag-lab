This toy corpus ships with the package. Unit tests and the tiny grid use it, so they need no download.

## Brewing green tea

Heat water to about eighty degrees, not boiling. Steep the green leaves for two minutes. Longer steeping makes the cup bitter because more tannins leave the leaf. Use about one teaspoon of leaves for each cup of water.

## Fixing a flat bicycle tire

Remove the wheel and lever the tire off the rim. Pull out the inner tube and pump some air into it. Listen for the hiss or dip the tube in water to find the bubbles. Patch the hole, let the glue dry, then refit the tube and inflate it to the pressure printed on the tire wall.

## Planting tomato seedlings

Wait until the last frost has passed. Dig a deep hole and bury the stem up to the first leaves, because tomatoes grow roots along the buried stem. Water the seedlings well and add a stake so the plant has support as the fruit gets heavy.

## Backing up a laptop

Copy your files to an external drive once a week. Keep a second copy somewhere else, such as a cloud folder or a friend's house. Test a restore now and then, because a backup that never restores is not a backup. Encrypt the drive if it holds private files.

## Training for a first marathon

Build up the weekly distance slowly, by about ten percent each week. Do one long run each weekend and keep the other runs easy. Rest days let the legs recover. Practice drinking water and eating gels during the long runs before race day.

## Sourdough starter care

Feed the starter flour and water every day while it lives on the counter. It is ready when it doubles in size a few hours after feeding and smells sour. In the fridge it needs feeding only once a week. Discard part of it before each feeding so the jar does not overflow.

## Cleaning a cast iron pan

Rinse the pan with hot water and scrub off stuck food with coarse salt. Skip long soaks because they cause rust. Dry the pan on the stove, then rub a thin layer of oil over the surface while it is warm to keep the seasoning.

## Learning to juggle three balls

Start with one ball and toss it from hand to hand at eye height. Add a second ball and throw it when the first reaches its peak. With three balls keep the same rhythm and let each throw make room for the next catch. Practice over a bed so drops do not roll away.
